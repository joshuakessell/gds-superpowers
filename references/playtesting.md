# Gameplay experiments and balance

## Evidence categories

A persona predicts. A simulation exercises rules. A visual capture exposes presentation. A human participant reports and demonstrates experience. Keep the categories distinct in every report.

## Experiment card

Before the session, define the target question, competing designs, expected observable behavior, participants, build/seed, procedure, measures, stop conditions, and decision rule. Identify known biases and accessibility needs. A small initial sample is exploratory and must not be presented as statistically representative.

Example hypothesis: players can distinguish a defensive and a synergy-building upgrade, explain their selection, and notice its effect within the next encounter. Failure could mean unreadable copy, weak feedback, negligible effects, or a poorly designed offer. The remedy depends on observation.

## Useful observations

Record time and confusion around a choice; offered options and current build; player explanation; whether behavior changes after selection; cause-of-death understanding; voluntary desire for another run; knowledge of what was lost/retained; and the next unlock the participant actually wants.

Questions should be neutral. Prefer 'What did you expect to happen?' over 'Was that upgrade exciting?' Distinguish spontaneous comments from prompted responses. Do not let an agent invent quotes, complete missing survey responses, or turn imagined players into a sample.

## Balance interpretation

Selection rate is conditional on what was offered, prerequisites, current build, player experience, and encounter context. A low pick rate alone does not prove an upgrade is weak. High win rates may reflect skilled users or prior unlocks. Separate choice accessibility, comprehension, power, opportunity cost, and synergy availability.

For economies, inspect resource sources/sinks, time to a meaningful option, unused currencies, compulsory grinding, and whether banking decisions vary by situation. For difficulty, examine readable threats, avoidable damage, encounter variance, and early-run viability without accumulated power.

Collect only necessary playtest data with participant knowledge. Local anonymous session IDs are usually enough for a prototype. Do not implement external analytics automatically.

## Iteration

Choose one major hypothesis per experiment and change as few explanatory variables as practical. Save the previous build so differences can be tested. End with retain, revise, remove, or gather more evidence. Maintain unresolved dissent and avoid pretending a small test proves market success.

The two-run slice must demonstrate understanding of loss/retention and a meaningful new possibility in run two. A purchase that only raises numbers is not sufficient evidence of the desired layered experience.
