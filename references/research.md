# Evidence and tool research

## Research brief before searching

Define a date window, platforms, relevant subgenres, approximate team size, and what counts as AI assistance or vibe coding for this investigation. The default window is the most recent 24 months plus older mechanical comparators, but record it as a proposed scope. Set a bounded initial cohort, for example 6 to 10 relevant cases, and expand only to resolve a material gap.

Do not promise to examine every game or identify a universally best formula. Public disclosures are incomplete. Report the search terms, inclusion criteria, exclusions, and unresolved coverage gaps.

## Separate two tracks

Gameplay track: compare combat pacing, temporary upgrades, build synergies, loot retention, base progression, onboarding, and player complaints. A successful conventional game can be a mechanics reference without any claim that it was AI-built.

Production track: verify which parts were AI-assisted, tools/versions, human responsibilities, testing, art cleanup, project scope, and release outcomes. Do not infer AI use from an art style, rapid schedule, marketing label, or missing development history.

Use primary evidence for development/tool claims: developer posts, source repositories, official release pages, talks, or direct statements. Player reviews are primary evidence of those players' reported experiences, not proof of their cause or representative retention.

## Evidence ledger

For every externally sourced claim record: claim ID; exact claim; source URL or repository reference; author/publisher; publication date when available; event/release date; access date; relevant passage or data; observation versus inference; uncertainty; and contradiction notes. Distinguish source dates from event dates.

Track these outcomes separately:

- Production: a playable or released artifact exists.
- Reception: review count, rating, sample selection, and observation date.
- Commercial: verified units, revenue, costs, or profit, with definitions and disclosure quality.
- Engagement: measured usage/retention or openly acknowledged absence of data.
- Attention: views, posts, downloads, wishlists, or publicity, not sales.

Mark missing figures `unknown`. Never convert review counts to sales without a clearly labeled model and substantial justification; avoid such estimates for design decisions when unnecessary.

Include counterexamples and mixed/negative cases. Consider price, marketing, distribution, timing, existing audience, team skill, and production quality as competing explanations. Similarity to a hit does not establish causation.

## Transfer patterns without cloning

For each proposed borrowed pattern: observed mechanism → evidence about reception → plausible benefit → uncertainty/confounders → implementation cost → original theme-specific expression → experiment.

Example hypothesis, not a market claim: readable three-choice rewards may help players commit to an evolving build. Test whether players can explain a choice and observe its effect. Do not copy another game's characters, names, exact assets, interface, or content catalog.

## Tool decision

Inspect the existing repository and developer strengths first. Compare no more than two or three viable stacks using a minimal export/test spike. Consider target platform, 2D/3D needs, editor overhead, language familiarity, automated/headless tests, profiling, art pipeline, licensing, packaging, accessibility, and distribution.

Do not choose a tool simply because an admired game used it. Do not migrate an existing engine without evidence of a blocker and an approved migration plan. Record exact versions after selection and verify current API/documentation before implementing.

Use the model and asset tools already accessible when adequate. Optional game-asset, visual-debugging, and performance skills should be discovered at runtime and invoked only for their actual supported scope. Generation and asset-admission/license checks remain separate tasks. Paid providers require an approved spending limit.

## Stop rule

Stop research when the prioritized decisions have sufficient evidence or the approved research budget expires. Return unresolved uncertainty and the cheapest validating prototype. Do not use ongoing research as a reason to avoid building the agreed experiment.
