# Example theme: The Drowned Light

## Theme (one sentence)
A lighthouse keeper rows out from a failing beacon to salvage a drowned archipelago, bringing back only what the lamp can be fed with.

## Mapping onto the core contract
| Contract element | In this theme |
|---|---|
| Base | The lighthouse: lamp room, workshop, harbor, and a chart table |
| Begin a run | Row out at dusk on a chosen route while the lamp burns |
| One of three temporary upgrades | At each wreck, the tide offers three salvaged fittings; the keeper can mount only one, and it is lost when the boat sinks or returns |
| Loot | Cargo, driftwood, brass, and glass pulled from wrecks |
| Secure eligible resources | Lash cargo to the boat's tow-line; only lashed items (not what the keeper is carrying loose) survive a capsizing |
| Death or finish | The boat sinks, or the keeper rows home before the lamp gutters |
| Temporary powers reset | Mounted fittings are stripped by the sea at run end |
| Retained resources fund | Lamp lenses and the workshop (facilities), a ferryman and a glassblower (specialists), new hull and tool families (equipment), new channels on the chart (routes), and fitting pairings (combinations) |

## Visual-bible sketch
- Palette mood: cold slate and sea-green shadows, lit by one warm lamp amber. Amber means "safe, owned, yours".
- Shape language: tall verticals and gentle arcs for the lighthouse and base; jagged diagonals for wrecks and hazards.
- Always: every light source in the world is amber or its reflection. Always draw salvage with a visible wear line from the water.
- Never: no pure black outlines, no neon or saturated colors outside amber, no clean unweathered metal.
- Never: no UI panel without the brass-and-rope frame, so base screens and run screens read as one world.

## Two sample upgrade families
Family access is not a guaranteed upgrade. Unlocking a family at the base adds that family's fittings to the pool of possible offers; it does not place any fitting in the keeper's boat at the start of a run, and each offer of three is still drawn from the pool at random with the choice limited to one.

### Family: Lenses (unlocked by the glassblower)
- Fresnel Shard: lantern beam briefly stuns light-averse creatures in a cone.
- Storm Prism: beam bends around one obstacle per sweep.
- Deep Mirror: reflects one hazard back each wreck.
Access rule: with the Lenses family unlocked, a wreck may offer any 0 to 3 of these fittings among its three options. A run can finish without seeing one.

### Family: Hulls (unlocked by the ferryman)
- Reed Skiff: faster rowing, shallow draft, fragile.
- Barnacle Plate: slower, absorbs one collision per wreck.
- Split Keel: carries a second tow-line slot for lashed cargo.
Access rule: unlocking the Hulls family lets the ferryman offer a hull to take out, but a chosen hull is a run-start equipment choice, never a free extra pick from the one-of-three.

## What this example does not claim
It has not been playtested, balanced, rendered, or built. The names, numbers, and fittings are placeholders for illustrating how a theme maps onto the contract.
